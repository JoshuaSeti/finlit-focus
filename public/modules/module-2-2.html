Opening
The Opening Problem

Natasha compared her rent to a mortgage payment and stopped there. She never asked what buying actually costs beyond the monthly figure.

Housing and vehicles are the two largest purchases most people ever make. Both are usually decided on gut feeling and a single monthly number — when the full picture is far more calculable than it looks.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening (4 min)
Opening question: "If your rent is K3,000/month and a mortgage payment on a similar house is also K3,000/month, is buying automatically the better deal?" Most say yes immediately. This module shows why that comparison is incomplete.

Key connection: Module U1 already covered the real cost of buying property (8% Property Transfer Tax, legal fees). This module applies that same true-cost thinking to the rent-vs-buy decision and to vehicle ownership.
N
Natasha Mumba
29 · Accountant · Lusaka
Natasha's rent was K3,200/month. A similar house would cost K650,000 with a 20% deposit and a mortgage payment close to K3,200/month — seemingly identical.

What she hadn't calculated: the K130,000 deposit, the roughly K65,000 in Property Transfer Tax and legal fees due immediately at purchase, and the fact that a mortgage is a 20-year commitment while rent can end with a month's notice.

The monthly number told her almost nothing about the real decision. This module gives you the tools to see the whole picture — for housing, and for the other major purchase most people underestimate: a vehicle.
🎯 By end of this module:(1) Calculate the true break-even point between renting and buying, including upfront costs. (2) Calculate the full annual cost of owning a vehicle, not just its price. (3) Apply a structured framework to any major purchase decision before committing.

Core Concept 2

The True Annual Cost of Owning a Vehicle

The sticker price is the beginning of the cost, not the whole of it. Fuel, insurance, maintenance, and depreciation continue every single year you own the car.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — True Cost of Ownership (6 min)
Depreciation is the cost people forget most. A car losing 15% of its value every year is a real cost, even though no cash changes hands in that specific transaction — walk through why this still matters for net worth.

Encourage students to plug in a car they've actually seen advertised — the exercise lands harder with a real, specific number.

Key Terms
Break-Even Point
The year cumulative buying costs drop below cumulative rent costs.
Closing Costs
Property Transfer Tax (8%) + legal/conveyancing fees, due upfront at purchase.
Depreciation
The annual decline in an asset's value — a real cost even without a sale.
Total Cost of Ownership
Purchase price plus all ongoing costs: fuel, insurance, maintenance, depreciation.
Equity
The portion of a property's value you actually own, growing as you pay down a mortgage.
