Core Concept 1

How the LuSE Is Actually Structured

The LuSE is a self-regulatory organisation with two distinct markets, specific rules about who must register with the SEC, and no OTC market.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Market Structure (8 min)
The 50-shareholder rule is the most practically relevant fact for future entrepreneurs: If your company reaches 50 shareholders, Zambian law classifies you as a public company and requires SEC registration. This catches many fast-growing SMEs by surprise.

No OTC market: Unlike the US, Zambia has no informal trading market. If shares are registered with the SEC, they must trade on a recognised exchange.
The Two-Tier Market
🏛️
Tier 1 — The Official List (Main Board)
Fully listed companies · Highest standards
Minimum public free-float of 25%. Requirements include 3 years of audited financial statements with unqualified opinions, board independence, and continuous disclosure obligations.

Listed here: Zambia Sugar · ZANACO · CEC Africa · Airtel Zambia · Standard Chartered Zambia · REIZ · Zambeef · British American Tobacco Zambia · Chilanga Cement · PUMA Energy
🚀
Tier 2 — LuSE Alt-M (Alternative Market)
Growth companies · SMEs · Lower barriers
Created for growth-stage companies and SMEs. Lower listing fees and requirements — still high governance standards.

Tier 3 — Registered with SEC but Not Yet Listed
Companies can register with the SEC and trade without being formally "listed."

The 50-shareholder rule: If a company reaches 50+ shareholders, it's automatically classified as public and must register with the SEC.

Core Concept 2

How a Company Lists on the LuSE — and Two Real Case Studies

From the first decision to ring the bell — here is the full path to listing, followed by two real 2025–2026 listings.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — IPO Mechanics (8 min)
Offer for Sale: Existing shareholders sell shares — no new money enters the company.
Offer for Subscription: The company issues brand new shares — money goes directly into the company.
The Path to Listing — Step by Step
1
Appoint a LuSE-licensed Sponsoring Broker
Conducts due diligence, structures the offer, drafts the prospectus, sets the share price.
2
Prepare 3 Years of Audited Financial Statements
All three years must have unqualified (clean) audit opinions.
3
Prepare a 2-Year Forward Business Plan
Must include financial projections, key assumptions, and risk factors.
4
Implement Required Governance Policies
Formal insider trading and risk management policies before listing.
5
Choose Your Offer Type
Offer for Sale (existing shareholders exit) or Offer for Subscription (fresh capital raised).
6
SEC Review and LuSE Approval
Prospectus review typically takes several weeks to months.
7
Public Offer Opens — Shares Allotted — Trading Begins
If oversubscribed, shares are allocated proportionally.

🔍 Tell me more — is 35.3x actually expensive?
What P/E actually means: a P/E of 35.3x means investors are paying K35.30 for every K1 of current annual profit. Put another way — at this profit level, it would take about 35 years of DCZ's current earnings to "pay back" your purchase price.

Is that high? For a mature, stable company, yes — quite high. But DCZ isn't mature: revenue grew 147% year-on-year. The market may be pricing in future growth, not today's thin 0.66% profit margin. High P/E can mean "overpriced" or "priced for rapid future growth" — the ratio alone can't tell you which.

Why this matters for you: never judge a share price in isolation. K12.30 sounds cheap. K35.30 per K1 of profit sounds expensive. Both describe the exact same share — the P/E ratio is what turns a price into an actual judgement.

What 114% oversubscription means in practice
For every ZMW 100,000 worth of shares offered, ZMW 214,000 was applied for. Chisomo — from our opening story — had ZMW 6,150 ready. If she applied for 500 shares, she might have received only 234 shares. The lesson: apply for more than you want when you expect oversubscription.

✅ Correct! This catches many fast-growing SMEs by surprise.

Key Terms
Official List
LuSE main board. Minimum 25% public free-float.
LuSE Alt-M
Alternative Market for growth companies. Minimum 10% free-float.
P/E Ratio
Price ÷ Earnings Per Share. Turns a share price into a judgement on value.
EPS
Earnings Per Share = Net Profit ÷ Total Shares Outstanding.
Offer for Subscription
New shares issued — fresh capital enters the company.
Offer for Sale
Existing shareholders sell — no new capital raised.
Direct Listing
Existing shares listed without a public offer. No new capital raised.
Oversubscription
When IPO demand exceeds shares on offer. DCZ was 114% oversubscribed.

Zero on LuSE share transactions in Zambia — a major structural advantage.
50-Shareholder Rule
