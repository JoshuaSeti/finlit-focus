Your Journey

You have covered 12 weeks of financial literacy. Now it all comes together.

This final module has two purposes: review every key concept across all four phases, and build your integrated personal financial plan — a document you will actually use after today.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening — Programme Completion (5 min)
This is the final session. Treat it as a celebration and a beginning.

Open by asking students to look back at the very first session: "What did you know about PAYE, compound interest, insurance, or investing before FinLit Zambia?" Let 3–4 students share. The contrast between then and now is the most powerful thing in the room.

Key framing for the session: Knowledge without action is just trivia. The goal of today is not to review content — it is to leave with a written plan that starts tomorrow. Every student should leave with at least one specific financial commitment on paper.

Phase 1 & 2 Review

Foundations and Wealth Building — Key Principles

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Foundations Review (10 min)
Run this as a rapid-fire group recall — not a lecture. Call out a topic and ask the group to give you the key number or principle before you show it:

• "What percentage is the PAYE-free monthly threshold?" → K5,100 of gross income
• "What is NAPSA?" → 5% employee + 5% employer, ceiling K34,164, max K1,708.20 — since 2018, no longer reduces PAYE
• "Rule of 72 — what does it tell you?" → Years to double = 72 ÷ interest rate
• "What's the single most important savings habit?" → Pay yourself first — automatic transfer on payday
• "TransUnion CRB — how do you check your score?" → *707# (Menyesha)

Any gaps — topics where no one in the room can answer — flag them. Those are the modules worth returning to individually.
💰
Phase 1 — The Non-Negotiables
Always budget from net income — not the salary on your offer letter.
The deduction order: Gross → PAYE applied directly to gross → NAPSA (5%) and NHIMA (1% basic) both deducted independently, neither affecting PAYE (confirmed via ZRA — this changed on 1 Jan 2018).
The 50/30/20 rule starts you. Your goal is to grow the 20% over time.
Compound interest rewards time more than amount — start now with whatever you have.
The Rule of 72: 72 ÷ interest rate = years to double your money.
Credit builds history — borrow wisely, repay always, check your TransUnion CRB at *707#.
Emergency fund first: 3 months of essential expenses before any other savings goal.
📈
Phase 2 — Building and Protecting
Insurance priority: Legal first (motor third-party + NHIMA) → catastrophic risks → emergency fund alongside.
Saving vs. investing: Savings for short-term and emergency. Investing for 3+ year goals.
The investment ladder: Emergency fund → money market fund → T-bills → government bonds → LuSE.
2025 verified rates: 91-day T-bill ~11.5% p.a. · 10-year GRZ bond ~16.6% p.a. · Access Bank savings 6% p.a.
GRZ bond minimum: K5,000. T-bill minimum: K1. Both require a CSD account at any commercial bank.
Any "investment" promising 30–50% monthly returns is fraud. Verify all investments at sec.gov.zm.

Phase 3 & 4 Review

Advanced Tools and Long-term Security

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Advanced Tools Review (10 min)
Continue the rapid-fire recall format for Phase 3 and 4 content:

• "T-Bills rate — approximate 2025 figure?" → ~11.5% p.a. (91-day)
• "GRZ Bonds — approximate 2025 rate?" → ~16.6% p.a. (5-year)
• "Name one cognitive bias and one system to counter it" → e.g. Present bias → automatic transfer
• "What is the SIM swap prevention step?" → SIM port PIN via MTN 100 / Airtel 111
• "NAPSA beneficiary — how do you name one?" → *677#
• "What does SDL stand for and who pays it?" → Skills Development Levy · employer only · 0.5%

Estate planning check: Ask "How many of you have a named NAPSA beneficiary?" Very few hands usually go up. Make this the first action item — it takes 2 minutes on *677# and protects years of contributions.
📊
Phase 3 — Advanced Tools
PAYE 2025 bands (applied directly to gross): K0–K5,100 (0%) · K5,101–K7,100 (20%) · K7,101–K9,200 (30%) · K9,200+ (37%).
Effective rate only — not marginal. Total PAYE ÷ Gross = the real percentage you pay.
NAPSA 2025: 5% employee + 5% employer · ceiling K34,164 · max contribution K1,708.20/month · does NOT reduce PAYE since 2018.
ZRA deadlines: PAYE by 10th · VAT by 18th · TOT by 14th · NAPSA by 10th · NHIMA by 10th.
The 6 cognitive traps: Gambler's fallacy · Loss aversion · Sunk cost · Present bias · Mental accounting · Social proof. Systems beat willpower every time.
Digital security: Different PINs for phone, MoMo, and bank. SIM port protection on. Transaction alerts on. Never share OTPs.
🌾
Phase 4 — Long-term Security
The three stages: Accumulation (18–50) · Preservation (50–60) · Distribution (60+).
NAPSA alone is insufficient: Most earners receive K1,500–K2,500/month from NAPSA at retirement. Private savings close the gap.
Starting at 20 vs. 40: K200/month at 7% annual return builds K608,741 from age 20 but only K124,929 from age 40. The 20-year difference costs K483,812.
Estate planning: Name a beneficiary on your NAPSA account at enapsa.napsa.co.zm or *677# — bypasses court delays entirely.
Without a will: The Intestate Succession Act (Cap. 59) distributes your estate — possibly not as you would want.

Phase 3 — PAYE
You earn K8,000 gross/month. PAYE is applied directly to gross (2025 bands: 0% to K5,100 · 20% to K7,100 · 30% to K9,200 · 37% above). What is your PAYE?
❌ K8,000 is above the K5,100 threshold. Multiple bands apply above that point.
✅ Correct! Band 2 (K5,100.01–K7,100 = K2,000): K2,000 × 20% = K400. Band 3 (K7,100.01–K8,000 = K900): K900 × 30% = K270. Total PAYE: K670. Effective rate: K670 ÷ K8,000 = 8.4%.
❌ This was correct before 1 January 2018. Since then, PAYE applies directly to the full K8,000 gross — not a reduced figure.
❌ 20% applies only to the K2,000 slice between K5,101–K7,100 — not to the full amount.
2
Phase 2 — Compound Interest
You save K200/month in a money market fund at 12% annual return for 5 years. Approximately how much will you have?
❌ K12,000 is what you deposited. The 12% annual return grows the fund above your deposits.
❌ A 12% annual return increases your balance — it never reduces it.
✅ Correct! At 12% annual (1% monthly) for 60 months: approximately K16,350. You deposited K12,000 — compound interest added approximately K4,350.
❌ Compound interest doesn't work by simple multiplication — returns earn their own returns each month.
3
Phase 3 — Behavioural Economics
Which cognitive bias causes people to continue investing in a failing business because they have "already put in so much money"?
✅ Correct! The money already invested is gone regardless of your next decision. Only future costs and returns are relevant.
❌ Loss aversion is about feeling losses more strongly than gains — not specifically about past investment driving future decisions.
❌ Present bias is about preferring immediate rewards over future ones.
❌ Gambler's Fallacy applies to random independent events — not to past-investment-driven reasoning.
4
Phase 4 — Estate Planning
What is the fastest way to protect your NAPSA funds so a named person receives them after your death, without court delays?
❌ A will must go through probate — which takes time and involves court.
❌ Verbal instructions have no legal standing for NAPSA funds.
✅ Correct! A named beneficiary receives funds directly — no court, no probate, no delays. Takes under 5 minutes.
❌ PACRA is for business registration — no role in NAPSA benefit distribution.
5
Phase 3 — Digital Security
You receive an SMS: "Your ZRA account has been flagged. Click here to verify: zra-zambia-secure.com." What do you do?
❌ This is a phishing attack. "zra-zambia-secure.com" is not a legitimate ZRA domain.
❌ The number is controlled by the scammer — calling confirms your number is active.
❌ Replying confirms your number is active. Never engage with suspicious SMS messages.
✅ Correct! Official ZRA communications come from zra.org.zm. Never click links from SMS or WhatsApp — always type the address directly.
