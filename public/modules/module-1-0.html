Opening Story
The Opening Problem

Natasha got the job offer she wanted. Her first payslip still surprised her.

A job offer quotes a number. The number on your first payslip is always smaller — and understanding exactly why is the first real skill of financial literacy.

N
Natasha Mwansa
22 · First job · Lusaka
Natasha was offered K3,500 per month as an administrative assistant. She had already planned how to spend it — rent, transport, a little saved each month. On payday, her account showed K3,290. "Where did K210 go?" she asked her older cousin. The answer — NAPSA, NHIMA, and PAYE — is where this module begins.
💡 Why This MattersEvery formally employed Zambian sees this exact gap between gross and net salary. Understanding it isn't optional — it's the foundation everything else in this module builds on.

Core Concept 1

Gross Pay, Deductions, and Net Pay

Three numbers matter on every Zambian payslip. Once you understand what each deduction is for, the gap stops feeling like money disappearing — it becomes money working for your future.

🏛️
NAPSA — 5%
National Pension Scheme. Builds your retirement benefit. Capped at K1,708.20/month in 2026.
🏥
NHIMA — 1%
National Health Insurance. Employer matches your 1% with another 1%. Covers eligible health services.
📋
PAYE
Pay As You Earn income tax. Calculated directly on gross income, using ZRA's progressive bands.
🏛️
NAPSA
/ nap-suh /
National Pension Scheme Authority — the statutory body managing mandatory pension contributions in Zambia.
Every formally employed Zambian contributes 5% of gross salary, matched by their employer, capped at K1,708.20/month in 2026. This builds the retirement benefit you'll draw on decades from now.
NAPSA = min(Gross Salary × 5%, K1,708.20)
🏥
NHIMA
/ en-hee-mah /
National Health Insurance Management Authority — administers Zambia's mandatory national health insurance scheme.
You contribute 1% of gross salary, matched by your employer with another 1%. Like NAPSA, NHIMA does not reduce your income for PAYE purposes — both are calculated independently.
📋
PAYE
/ pay-as-you-earn /
Zambia's system for withholding income tax directly from an employee's salary, calculated on a progressive band structure.
The first K5,100 of monthly gross income is tax-free. Above that, rates rise in bands — 20%, then 30%, then 37% at the top. PAYE is calculated directly on gross income — since 1 January 2018, NAPSA contributions no longer reduce the PAYE base (confirmed via ZRA: zra.org.zm/tax-information).
👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Explaining Deductions (5 min)
Key facts to emphasise:
• NAPSA is deducted before PAYE is calculated — this order matters and often confuses first-time earners
• Neither NHIMA nor NAPSA reduce the PAYE base — PAYE applies to gross income directly, since a 2018 Income Tax Act amendment (a very common misconception worth correcting explicitly)
• The K5,100 threshold means many entry-level and part-time earners pay zero PAYE
• Ask learners to guess their own deduction percentage before revealing it — the surprise builds retention

Core Concept 3

SMART Financial Goals

"Save more money" is a wish. A SMART goal is specific enough to actually act on — and specific enough to know when you've reached it.

🎯
Specific
Exactly what you want
📏
Measurable
A real number
✅
Achievable
Realistic for you
🔗
Relevant
Actually matters
⏰
Time-bound
A real deadline
🎯
SMART Goal
/ smart gohl /
A goal structured to be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound — turning a wish into a plan you can actually act on.
"I want to save money" fails every test. "I will save K1,500 for a smartphone by saving K150/month, reaching my goal in 10 months" passes all five — and gives you something concrete to track.
👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Running the Goal Exercise (5 min)
Key facts to emphasise:
• Most learners' first attempt at a goal is vague ("save more") — the SMART structure forces specificity
• The Measurable step (a real kwacha number) is usually the hardest for first-time goal-setters
• Encourage learners to pick a goal that's genuinely theirs — it dramatically increases follow-through
• This activity's data is saved locally on their device only — reassure learners nothing is shared externally
