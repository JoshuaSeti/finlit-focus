The Opening Problem

Mwansa's fritter stall was busy every single day. She still couldn't tell you if it made any money.

A good business idea and a financially healthy business are two different things. This module gives you the specific tools — separation, break-even math, and real funding routes — that turn a busy stall into a business you can actually trust the numbers on.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening (4 min)
Opening question: "If you ran a small business today, could you tell me — right now — how many sales you need in a month just to break even?" Almost nobody can answer confidently. That gap is exactly what this module closes.

Connection to Module B1 (Starting a Business in Zambia): that module covers registration steps. This module covers what happens financially once the business is actually running.

Core Concept 1

Separate Business Money From Personal Money

This single habit is the difference between a business you can genuinely evaluate and one that's just a guess dressed up as a livelihood.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Separation (6 min)
The fix doesn't require a formal bank account on day one. Even two separate cash boxes — one for business, one for personal — creates the discipline. A dedicated mobile money wallet for business only is a realistic first step for most small vendors.

Introduce the term "owner's draw" — a deliberate, recorded transfer from business to personal funds, as opposed to an informal, undocumented grab from the till.

Core Concept 2

Break-Even Analysis — The Number Every Business Owner Should Know

Before any profit is real, you have to cover your costs. This one calculation tells you exactly how much you need to sell to get there.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Break-Even (8 min)
Fixed costs stay the same regardless of sales volume — rent, licence fees. Variable costs scale directly with each unit sold — ingredients, packaging.

After the Case, ask students to calculate their own break-even for a business idea of their choice — this is far more memorable applied to something they actually care about.

🔍 Tell me more — how is this actually calculated?
The formula: Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per unit). Each fritter sold at K5.00, costing K2.00 in ingredients, contributes K3.00 toward covering your fixed K450 rent and license cost.

The maths: K450 ÷ K3.00 = 150 fritters. Sell fewer than 150 in a month and you're operating at a loss — every fritter beyond 150 is genuine profit.

Why this matters for you: most small businesses fail not because the product is bad, but because the owner never calculated this number — and had no idea whether a "good sales day" was actually profitable or still underwater.

The Formula, Generalised
Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit). The denominator is called your "contribution margin" — how much each sale actually contributes toward covering fixed costs.

Core Concept 3

Real Sources of Startup Capital in Zambia

Beyond personal savings, several real, accessible funding routes exist — each with different requirements worth knowing before you need them.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Startup Capital (6 min)
Connect to Module 2.3's structural theme: just like agricultural credit, formal SME funding (CEEC) typically requires land-based collateral — a genuine barrier for many young entrepreneurs, not just a paperwork inconvenience.

Chilimba (informal savings/lending circles) remain a real, common source of small business capital across Zambia — worth acknowledging honestly rather than dismissing.
🏛️ CEEC — Citizens Economic Empowerment Commission
Established underCitizens Economic Empowerment Act No. 9 of 2006
Priority groupsYouth, women, persons with disabilities
Typical requirementLand-based collateral (or third-party guarantee)
🏦
NATSAVE — Small Business Loans
National Savings and Credit Bank offers SME-focused loan products — generally more accessible than commercial bank lending for smaller loan amounts.
🚀
BongoHive and Innovation Hubs
Lusaka-based accelerator supporting early-stage entrepreneurs and startups — mentorship, sometimes seed funding access, particularly for tech-oriented businesses.
🤝
Chilimba — Informal Savings Circles
A rotating group savings arrangement, widely used across Zambia to raise small business capital without formal collateral requirements.
🎓
Youth Empowerment Fund
Administered through the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Arts — targeted specifically at youth-led business ventures.
📌 The honest patternJust like agricultural credit in Module 2.3, formal small business funding usually requires collateral most new entrepreneurs don't yet have. Starting with personal savings, chilimba, or a small NATSAVE loan — then building toward CEEC eligibility as the business grows — is often the realistic path, not a failure to plan ahead.

Key Terms
Owner's Draw
A deliberate, recorded transfer of money from business to personal use.
Fixed Costs
Costs that stay the same regardless of sales volume — rent, licence fees.
Variable Costs
Costs that scale directly with units sold — ingredients, packaging.
Break-Even Point
Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost). The sales volume needed to cover all costs.
Contribution Margin
Price minus variable cost per unit — how much each sale contributes to fixed costs.
CEEC
Citizens Economic Empowerment Commission. Typically requires land-based collateral.
Chilimba
Informal rotating savings circle, widely used for small business capital.
