Opening story
The Opening Problem

Mulenga got a K4,000 promotion. His bank account barely noticed.

Understanding why a salary increase does not produce an equal increase in take-home pay is one of the most practically useful things a Zambian worker can learn.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening (3 min)
Opening question: "If your salary doubled tomorrow, would your take-home pay also double?" Let 2–3 students answer before revealing the story. Most say yes.

Effective rate only: The 20%, 30%, 37% rates apply only to each income slice — not your full salary. Never say "you're in the 37% bracket" — it is misleading. Always use effective rate language.
M
Mulenga Mutale
28 years old · Financial analyst · Lusaka
Mulenga had earned K8,000/month for two years. He knew his payslip. Then came the promotion: K12,000.

He calculated in his head: K4,000 more. K48,000 extra per year.

His first payslip at K12,000 showed net pay of K9,214 — just K2,364 more than before.

"Where did K1,636 go?"

HR showed him: at K8,000, his PAYE was K670 (8.4% of gross). At K12,000, it jumped to K2,066 (17.2% of gross). NAPSA also rose by K200. The K4,000 gross became K2,364 net.

Mulenga had never been shown how to calculate this. This module changes that.

🔍 Tell me more — where did the K1,636 actually go?
The mechanism: Mulenga's raise pushed his gross income from K8,000 to K12,000 — crossing fully through the 30% band and deep into the 37% band. PAYE is calculated directly on gross income (not reduced by NAPSA, per a 2018 change to the Income Tax Act), and each slice within its own band is taxed separately.

Nothing was actually lost: the extra K200 NAPSA builds Mulenga's own pension. The extra K1,396 PAYE funds public services. K2,364 more in the pocket every month is still a genuinely good raise — the K1,636 "gap" was never money Mulenga was ever going to keep, it just wasn't obvious from the offer letter.

Why this matters for you: Before accepting any raise or new job, run the numbers past NAPSA and PAYE bands first — your real decision should be based on net pay, not the number on the offer.

Core concept
Core Concept 1

Zambia's 2025 PAYE Bands

Progressive taxation means only the income above each threshold is taxed at that band's rate. These bands apply from January 2025, verified from ZRA.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Bands (6 min)
Deduction order to drill: Gross → PAYE applied directly to gross → NAPSA (5%) and NHIMA (1%) both deducted independently, in either order, neither affecting PAYE.
Neither NAPSA nor NHIMA reduce the PAYE base — this has been true since 1 January 2018.

Common errors: Applying 20% to the entire salary instead of just the K5,101–K7,100 slice. Assuming NAPSA is deducted before PAYE (true before 2018, not anymore). Forgetting that PAYE now applies to gross directly.
📋
The Deduction Order — Always This Sequence
Step 1 → Gross Salary
Step 2 → Apply PAYE bands directly to Gross Salary
Step 3 → Deduct NAPSA (5%, capped at K1,708.20 — calculated independently, does NOT reduce PAYE)
Step 4 → Deduct NHIMA (1% of basic — also calculated independently)
Step 5 → Net Pay
Since 1 January 2018, following an Income Tax Act amendment, NAPSA no longer reduces the PAYE base — confirmed directly via zra.org.zm/tax-information.
2025 PAYE Bands — Applied Directly to Monthly Gross Income

0% Tax Free

Core Concept 2

NAPSA, NHIMA, and SDL

Three statutory deductions, three different rules. Confusing them is the single most common payroll mistake in Zambia.

NAPSA 2025 Quick Reference
Employee rate5% of gross
Employer rate5% matched
2025 earnings ceilingK34,164/month
Max contribution (each)K1,708.20
Reduces PAYE base?No — since 2018 ✗
Due date10th of month
Late penalty10% cumulative
🏥
NHIMA — National Health Insurance Management Authority
Employee: 1% of basic salary · Employer: 1% matched · No ceiling
Calculated entirely independently of PAYE — same as NAPSA, since 2018
Basic salary only — excludes overtime and allowances · Due: 10th
📚
SDL — Skills Development Levy
Employer only · 0.5% of gross emoluments
Applies above K800,000 annual turnover · Never deduct from employees
Tax-deductible from January 2025 · Due: 10th

⚖️
Tax Planning, Avoidance & Evasion — Know the Difference
Tax Planning (Proactive · Legal)Structuring decisions with tax consequences in mind — e.g. maximising allowable deductions before filing. Entirely within the law.

Tax Avoidance (Reactive · Legal)Using available legal provisions to reduce your overall liability — e.g. claiming allowable deductions correctly, or structuring income through legitimate means. This is your right as a taxpayer.

Tax Evasion (Illegal · Criminal)Deliberately hiding income, filing false returns, or failing to register. Criminal offence under Zambia's Income Tax Act, Chapter 323. Penalties include fines and imprisonment.
📌 A clear example of legal tax planningYour NAPSA contribution builds your own pension — a genuine, mandatory benefit. Note: since 1 January 2018, NAPSA no longer reduces your PAYE — it is calculated entirely independently. If you're looking for legal ways to reduce PAYE specifically, consult ZRA's current guidance on allowable deductions directly, as this changes periodically.

Key Terms
PAYE
Pay As You Earn. Zero below K5,100 gross income/month (2025). Progressive bands above, applied directly to gross.
Gross Income
Your full salary before deductions. PAYE bands apply directly here since 2018 — not to a reduced figure.
Effective Rate
Total PAYE ÷ Gross. The real percentage going to tax. Always lower than your top band rate.
NAPSA 2025
5%+5%. Ceiling K34,164. Max K1,708.20. Does NOT reduce the PAYE base — calculated independently since 2018.
NHIMA
1% of basic salary. Does NOT reduce the PAYE base — calculated independently, same as NAPSA.
SDL
0.5% gross. Employer only. Above K800K turnover. Never deduct from employees.
Tax Avoidance
Legally reducing tax liability using permitted means allowed under current ZRA guidance. Legal, distinct from evasion.
Tax Evasion
Illegal concealment of income. Criminal offence under Income Tax Act, Chapter 323.
ZRA
Zambia Revenue Authority. zra.org.zm · Call: 4000 (toll-free).
Tax Band
Income range taxed at a specific rate. Only the slice within the band is taxed at that rate.

Deduction Order
What is the correct deduction order on a Zambian payslip?
❌ This was correct before 1 January 2018. Since then, an Income Tax Act amendment means NAPSA no longer reduces the PAYE base.
✅ Correct! Since 1 January 2018, PAYE is calculated directly on gross income. NAPSA and NHIMA are both independent deductions that don't affect the PAYE calculation (source: zra.org.zm).
❌ Neither NHIMA nor NAPSA reduce the PAYE base — PAYE is calculated on gross income directly, independent of both.
❌ NAPSA is 5% of gross, calculated independently — not based on net pay.
2
Band Calculation
Gross salary K8,000. PAYE is calculated directly on gross. What is total PAYE?
❌ 20% applies only to the K2,000 slice between K5,101–K7,100. Not to the full K8,000.
✅ Correct! Band 1 (K0–K5,100) = K0. Band 2 (K5,101–K7,100) = K2,000 × 20% = K400. Band 3: K8,000−K7,100 = K900 × 30% = K270. Total K670. Effective rate 8.4%.
❌ This is the pre-2018 method. PAYE now applies to the full K8,000 gross, not K7,600.
❌ The tax-free threshold is K5,100 — K8,000 exceeds this, so PAYE applies.
3
NAPSA Ceiling
What is the 2025 NAPSA ceiling and maximum monthly employee contribution?
❌ NAPSA has a ceiling of K34,164/month. Above this, contributions are capped at K1,708.20.
✅ Correct! Anyone earning above K34,164/month contributes only K1,708.20 — 5% of the ceiling, not 5% of their full salary.
❌ 2025 ceiling is K34,164/month. Maximum contribution is K1,708.20.
❌ The verified 2025 figures are ceiling K34,164, maximum K1,708.20.
4
NHIMA Basis
NHIMA is calculated on which salary component — and does it affect the PAYE calculation?
❌ Both wrong. NHIMA is on basic salary only (not gross), and does NOT affect the PAYE calculation.
❌ NHIMA is 1% of basic salary, calculated independently — it plays no role in the PAYE calculation at all.
✅ Correct! NHIMA = 1% of basic salary. Excludes allowances and overtime. Like NAPSA, it has no effect on the PAYE calculation — both are calculated independently from gross income.
❌ NHIMA is on basic salary — not on net pay.
5
Effective Rate
At K12,000 gross, effective PAYE is 17.2%. Which statement is most accurate?
❌ The 37% rate applies only to the slice above K9,200. Effective rate is 17.2% — not 37%.
❌ Progressive taxation means different effective rates. K8,000: 8.4%. K12,000: 17.2%.
✅ Correct! Effective rate = K2,066 ÷ K12,000 = 17.2%. The 37% band applies only to the top K2,800 slice (K1,036 of PAYE) — not the whole salary.
❌ 17.2% is PAYE only. Full deductions = NAPSA K600 + PAYE K2,066 + NHIMA K120 = K2,786. Net = K9,214.
