Opening Story
The Opening Problem

Bwalya borrowed K2,000 from a payday lender. She still hasn't paid it off two years later.

She needed cash fast for a family emergency. She didn't check the rate. That single decision, made under pressure, cost her far more than the K2,000 she borrowed.

B
Bwalya Mumba
26 · Shop assistant · Ndola
Bwalya's mother needed emergency medical treatment. A payday lender offered K2,000 "same day, no questions asked" — she signed without reading the rate. At 35% per year, minimum payments barely touched the principal. Two years and several renewed loans later, she has paid back more than K4,500 — and still owes money. "I didn't understand what I was agreeing to," she says. This module exists so you never have to say that.
💡 Why This MattersCredit itself isn't dangerous — misunderstood credit is. Every Zambian will face a borrowing decision at some point. Understanding the real cost before you sign is the single most protective financial skill you can build.

Core Concept 1

What a Loan Really Costs

The advertised interest rate is only part of the story. The real question is always: how much total money will leave my pocket by the time this loan is fully repaid?

💳
Annual Interest Rate
/ AN-yoo-ul IN-ter-est rayt /
The cost of borrowing money for one year, expressed as a percentage of the amount borrowed.
Zambian lenders range widely — formal bank loans around 20%, microfinance institutions around 28%, and payday lenders reaching 35% or higher. The gap between these numbers, on the same K10,000, can mean thousands of kwacha in extra interest.
Total Interest = Principal × Rate × Years

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Explaining Loan Costs (5 min)
Key facts to emphasise:
• Learners often focus only on the monthly payment, not the total repayment — reframe toward total cost
• The gap between 20% and 35% on the same loan amount is dramatic — have learners compare side by side
• Emergency borrowing under pressure (like Bwalya's story) is exactly when people skip comparing rates — name this pattern explicitly
• Ask: "What would you do differently if you had 24 hours to compare three lenders instead of signing immediately?"

Core Concept 2

Avalanche vs Snowball — Which Strategy Wins?

If you have more than one debt, the order you pay them off in changes how much you pay in total, and how fast you become debt-free. Two competing strategies — let's test both.

⛰️
Avalanche Method
Pay off the highest-interest debt first. Mathematically saves the most money over time.
⛄
Snowball Method
Pay off the smallest balance first. Builds motivation through quick, visible wins.
