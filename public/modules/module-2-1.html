Opening
The Opening Problem

Chama kept all her savings in cash for three years. Inflation quietly ate a third of its value.

Saving protects what you have. Investing is what makes it grow faster than prices rise. This module walks the ladder from safest to riskiest — and gives you the exact tools to build a portfolio that matches your own risk tolerance.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening (4 min)
Opening question: "If you kept K1,000 in cash under your mattress for 3 years, would you still be able to buy the same things with it?" Most say yes — this is the exact misconception Chama's story corrects. Cash loses real value every year inflation exceeds 0%.

Key framing: This module doesn't push any single investment — it builds the judgment to compare risk and return across several real, accessible Zambian options.
C
Chama Musonda
26 · Nurse · Kabwe
Chama saved K400 every month for three years — K14,400 total, sitting safely in a savings account earning almost nothing. She felt responsible. Disciplined, even.

Then a colleague mentioned Treasury Bills — the same government-backed safety Chama valued, but actually earning a real return. Chama realised she hadn't lost her money to theft or fraud. She'd lost purchasing power to inflation, quietly, every single month, without ever noticing.

This module is about making your money's safety and its growth work together, not against each other.
🎯 By end of this module:(1) Compare risk and return across savings, Treasury Bills, government bonds, and LuSE shares. (2) Calculate a real T-Bill return including withholding tax. (3) Build and evaluate a diversified investment portfolio matched to your own risk tolerance.
💡 The Investment LadderEmergency fund (Module 1.3) → Treasury Bills → Government Bonds → LuSE Shares. Each step up trades some safety for higher expected return. Never skip the emergency fund step.

Core Concept 2

Treasury Bills — Government-Backed, Real Returns

T-Bills are short-term government debt — you're lending money to the Zambian government for a fixed period, at a fixed rate, with virtually no default risk.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — T-Bills (7 min)
Rates change every 2 weeks at auction — emphasise this explicitly. The rates shown are representative of the 2025-2026 range, not a permanent fixed number. Always check boz.zm for the live current rate before actually investing.

The withholding tax step is the part most people miss — a 15% tax applies to T-Bill interest for domestic individual investors. Walk through the gross-to-net calculation explicitly.
🏛️
4 Available Tenors
T-Bills come in 91-day, 182-day, 273-day, and 364-day terms. Longer terms typically pay higher rates, but lock your money up longer. Minimum investment: K1.

Core Concept 3

Build Your Own Diversified Portfolio

Diversification — spreading money across different risk levels — reduces your exposure to any single investment failing. Build your own mix and see the projected outcome.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Portfolio Building (7 min)
There's no single "correct" portfolio. A 19-year-old with 40 years to retirement can reasonably hold more LuSE exposure than someone 5 years from needing the money. Ask students to justify their own split, not just build one.

Return assumptions used here (savings ~6%, T-Bills ~14%, LuSE ~20%, property ~10%) are representative long-run figures, cross-checked against Module U2's verified LASI performance data and Module 4.1's cited savings rate — not guaranteed future returns.

Quick Fire — 5 Questions

0/5
1
Question 1 of 5
Why did Chama lose purchasing power even though her savings balance never decreased?
❌ The story is about inflation, not bank fees.
✅ Correct! Her balance stayed the same number, but that number could buy less every year.
❌ No scam occurred — this is the quiet, ordinary effect of inflation.
❌ Currency appreciation would work in the opposite direction.
2
Question 2 of 5
You invest K5,000 in a 182-day T-Bill at a 14% annualised rate. Approximately how much gross interest do you earn over the 182 days (before tax)?
❌ 14% is an annualised rate — you must adjust it for the actual 182-day holding period.
✅ Correct! The annualised rate is scaled down to the actual number of days you hold the bill.
❌ This treats the annual rate as if it applied to the full period, not adjusted for 182 days.
❌ All four tenors pay interest at maturity, including 182-day bills.
3
Question 3 of 5
What percentage withholding tax applies to T-Bill interest for a domestic individual investor?
❌ A 15% withholding tax applies — T-Bills are not tax-free.
✅ Correct! This is deducted from the interest earned, not the principal — always calculate your NET return.
❌ 30% is not the correct T-Bill withholding rate.
❌ T-Bill withholding tax is a separate, lower rate from PAYE bands.
4
Question 4 of 5
Why does diversification (spreading money across savings, T-Bills, and LuSE shares) reduce risk?
❌ Diversification manages risk — it doesn't guarantee higher returns.
✅ Correct! This is the core principle behind spreading risk across multiple asset types.
❌ No investment strategy eliminates risk completely.
❌ An emergency fund (Module 1.3) remains essential regardless of portfolio diversification.
5
Question 5 of 5
Someone offers an investment promising LuSE-level returns with savings-account-level safety and no risk of loss. What should you conclude?
❌ Risk and return move together — this combination doesn't exist legitimately.
✅ Correct! This is the exact same red flag covered in Module U4's crypto scam framework — applies to any investment claim.
❌ The mathematical impossibility of the claim doesn't change based on who's offering it.
❌ REITs (Module U1) don't offer this risk-free high-return combination either.

Key Terms
Treasury Bill (T-Bill)
Short-term government debt. 4 tenors: 91/182/273/364 days. Minimum K1.
Withholding Tax
15% tax deducted from T-Bill interest for domestic individual investors.
Diversification
Spreading investments across asset types to reduce exposure to any single failure.
Risk-Return Trade-off
Higher potential returns generally require accepting higher risk of loss.
Government Bond
Longer-term government debt than T-Bills, typically 2+ years.
Investment Ladder
Emergency fund → T-Bills → Bonds → LuSE Shares, increasing risk at each step.
