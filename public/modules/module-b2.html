Opening
The Opening Problem

Zindikila collected K112,000 in VAT from her clients. She remitted K0 to ZRA — because she thought it was hers.

This is one of the most common and costly business mistakes in Zambia's small business sector. This module covers exactly why it happens, and the real mechanics that prevent it.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Opening (2 min)
Opening question: "If a customer pays you K116 for a product that costs K100, who actually owns the K16?" Most students will say the business. The correct answer is ZRA — VAT is collected on ZRA's behalf.

Key framing: VAT is a consumption tax ultimately borne by the final customer. Every business in the chain collects it and passes it on, keeping only what they paid on their own inputs.

Connects to Module 3.0: that module covered the K800,000 VAT registration threshold. This module covers what happens after registration — the actual mechanics of operating as a VAT-registered business.
👩🏾‍💼
Zindikila Magwende
28 · Events catering business · Lusaka
Zindikila ran a catering business from her home in Chelstone. After three years of growth, her annual revenue crossed K800,000. Her accountant told her she needed to register for VAT.

She registered. She started adding "VAT 16%" to her invoices. Clients paid. Business was booming.

Eighteen months later, a ZRA officer arrived. Zindikila had collected K112,000 in VAT from clients over that period. She had remitted K0 to ZRA. She thought the 16% was extra income.

The penalty: back-taxes of K112,000 plus a K22,400 penalty (20% of unpaid amount) plus interest exceeding K12,000. Total liability: over K134,000.

"I was charging it. I just didn't know I was supposed to send it to ZRA," she said.
💡 The Core PrincipleVAT is not your income. When you charge a customer 16% VAT, you are collecting a government tax on ZRA's behalf. You hold it temporarily, deduct what you paid on your own business purchases, and remit the difference to ZRA by the 18th of every month.

Core Concept 1

Output VAT, Input VAT, and the Supply Chain

VAT is charged at every stage of the supply chain — but each business only pays the net amount, on the value it actually added.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — The Chain (10 min)
The key insight: VAT is not cumulative — it does not stack on itself. Tax at each stage is calculated only on the value added at that stage, which is why it's called Value Added Tax.

Common student confusion: "Why register for VAT if you have to pay it to ZRA?" Answer: because you also claim back all the VAT you paid on business purchases — which unregistered businesses cannot do.
📤
Output VAT — What You Charge
The VAT you add to your sales invoices — 16% at the standard rate. Not your money; held on behalf of ZRA.

Formula: Output VAT = Sales (excl. VAT) × 16%
Example: Sell catering for K10,000 → Output VAT = K1,600. Total invoice = K11,600.
📥
Input VAT — What You Recover
VAT you paid on your own business purchases. Because you're VAT-registered, ZRA lets you deduct this from your output VAT.

Formula: Input VAT = Purchases (excl. VAT) × 16%
Example: Bought ingredients for K4,000 → Input VAT = K640 reclaimable.
💳
VAT Payable — What Goes to ZRA
VAT Payable = Output VAT − Input VAT

Using the examples above: K1,600 − K640 = K960 payable to ZRA.

If input VAT exceeds output VAT in a month, you have a VAT credit — carried forward, or claimed as a refund under Section 40 of the VAT Act.
VAT Through the Supply Chain — Maize Flour Example
1. Farmer sells maize to millerOutput VAT: K0 (zero-rated)
Sale price K5,000. Raw agricultural produce is zero-rated. Miller pays K5,000 only.
↓
2. Miller sells flour to wholesalerOutput VAT: K1,120
Sells for K7,000 + 16% VAT = K8,120. Input VAT from maize: K0. Miller remits K1,120.
↓
3. Wholesaler sells to retailerOutput VAT: K1,440
Sells for K9,000 + VAT = K10,440. Input VAT paid: K1,120. Wholesaler remits K320.
↓
4. Retailer sells to final customerOutput VAT: K1,760
Sells for K11,000 + VAT = K12,760. Input VAT paid: K1,440. Retailer remits K320.
↓
5. Final customer
Pays K12,760 total — bearing the full K1,760 VAT cost. ZRA's total collection across the whole chain: K1,120 + K320 + K320 = K1,760 — exactly matching what the final customer paid. Nothing is double-counted.

Core Concept 2

Standard-Rated, Zero-Rated, and Exempt — Not the Same Thing

Zero-rated and exempt both mean "no VAT charged to the customer" — but for the business itself, they're completely different.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Categories (8 min)
Zambia-specific items worth emphasising: exports are always zero-rated (critical for any student considering an export business); agricultural inputs are zero-rated; financial services (bank charges, insurance) are exempt — students often assume banks charge VAT on everything.
CategoryRateClaim Input VAT?Examples
Standard16%✅ YesMost goods/services — retail, catering, professional services, construction, transport, telecoms
Zero-Rated0%✅ Yes — full recoveryExports · Agricultural inputs · Basic foodstuffs (bread, oil, milk, salt) · Medical drugs · Books · Domestic electricity/water
ExemptNo VAT❌ No — becomes a costFinancial services (banking, insurance) · Residential rental · Education fees · Medical services · Public transport
⚠️
Zero-Rated vs. Exempt — The Critical Difference
Zero-rated (e.g. food exporter): Charges 0% VAT, still claims back 16% on all inputs — often receives monthly refunds.

Exempt (e.g. private school): Charges no VAT, cannot claim back any input VAT — it becomes a cost absorbed into fees.

A business making both must apportion input VAT — only the taxable-supply portion is reclaimable.
🔢
VAT Registration Threshold
Mandatory: Annual taxable turnover above K800,000 — register within 30 days. Failure is a criminal offence under the VAT Act, Cap 331.

Voluntary: Available below K800,000 if beneficial — e.g. significant input VAT to recover, or VAT-registered customers needing your invoices.
📌 Partial ExemptionIf your business makes both taxable and exempt supplies, you can only recover the input VAT portion related to your taxable supplies — an apportionment calculation, typically based on the ratio of taxable to total turnover.

Core Concept 3

Calculating VAT Payable and Filing Your Return

Every VAT-registered business files a monthly return and remits VAT payable by the 18th of the following month.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Calculation & Filing (10 min)
Smart Invoice is now mandatory: from January 2025, paper invoices are no longer accepted as valid VAT invoices. Input VAT claims are rejected without a Smart Invoice receipt from the supplier.

Records: must be kept a minimum of 6 years — same as other ZRA records.

Smart Invoice — Mandatory Since January 2025
1
Register on Smart Invoice
Via zra.org.zm → TaxOnline. You receive a unique taxpayer QR code.
2
Issue every invoice through the system
Every sale generates a Smart Invoice receipt, recorded with ZRA in real time.
3
Only Smart Invoice receipts qualify for input VAT claims
A handwritten receipt from a supplier can't be claimed against.
4
File by the 18th, keep records 6 years
TaxOnline auto-populates from Smart Invoice transactions.
📅
Penalty Summary
Late filing: K2,500/month overdue · Late payment: 2% monthly interest · Failure to register: criminal offence, back-VAT assessed · Non-Smart Invoice: input claim disallowed.

Zindikila's penalty was 20% of unpaid VAT plus 2% monthly interest — for K112,000 unremitted over 18 months, this exceeded K34,000 before the back-tax itself.

A business has output VAT of K4,800 and input VAT of K1,920 this month. How much does it remit to ZRA?
❌ The business can deduct input VAT paid — remitting the full amount means overpaying.
❌ Input VAT is deducted from output VAT, not remitted on its own.
✅ Correct! K4,800 − K1,920 = K2,880 — the net amount after recovering what was paid on purchases.
❌ Adding the two has no basis in VAT law — the formula is always subtraction.

Question 3 of 6
What is the critical difference between a zero-rated supply and an exempt supply?
❌ True for the customer, but completely different for the business — input VAT recovery is the key difference.
❌ There is no 8% rate in Zambia's VAT system.
✅ Correct! An exporter (zero-rated) recovers all input VAT, often getting refunds. A school (exempt) absorbs input VAT as a cost.
❌ This is backwards — exempt-only businesses may not even need to register.

Key Terms
Output VAT
VAT charged on your sales. Belongs to ZRA — collected and remitted monthly.
Input VAT
VAT paid on your business purchases. Reclaimable against output VAT.
VAT Payable
Output VAT minus Input VAT. Remitted by the 18th of the following month.
VAT Credit / Refund
When input VAT exceeds output VAT — carried forward or refunded (VAT Act s.40).
Standard-Rated
16% VAT — most goods and services.
Zero-Rated
0% VAT on sales, full input VAT recovery. Exports, basic foods, agri-inputs.
Exempt
No VAT charged, no input VAT recovery. Financial services, education, medical.
Smart Invoice
ZRA's mandatory electronic invoicing system since January 2025.
