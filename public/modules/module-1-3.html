Opening Story
The Opening Problem

Chanda lost her job on a Friday. She had zero days of runway.

She wasn't reckless with money — she paid her bills, avoided debt, even saved sometimes. But every kwacha had a job the moment it arrived. When the shock came, she had nothing standing between her and crisis borrowing.

C
Chanda Banda
24 · Retail assistant · Livingstone
Chanda's employer closed the store without warning. She had no savings set aside specifically for this — everything had already been allocated to rent, food, and family support. Within a week she had borrowed from a kaloba lender at a punishing rate just to cover the gap. The problem wasn't her spending. It was that she never had a fund built specifically for exactly this moment.
💡 Why This MattersAn emergency fund is not the same as savings for a goal. It has one job — sitting untouched until a genuine shock hits, so that a job loss, medical bill, or urgent repair never forces you into expensive debt.
🛟
Emergency Fund
/ i-MUR-jun-see fund /
A cash reserve set aside specifically to cover unexpected expenses — medical costs, job loss, or urgent repairs — without relying on debt.
The common guideline is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses. But even a small starter fund of one month significantly reduces vulnerability — it doesn't need to be complete to already be useful.

Core Concept 1

How Much Is Enough?

"Save more" is not a target. A real emergency fund target is a specific number, based on what you actually spend on essentials each month — not your full lifestyle spending, just what keeps you afloat.

🏠
Essential Expenses Only
Rent, food, transport, utilities — not entertainment or discretionary spending.
💧
Stay Liquid
Keep it in a savings account or mobile money wallet — accessible in days, not locked away.

👩‍🏫 Facilitator Note — Setting a Realistic Target (5 min)
Key facts to emphasise:
• A 6-month target can feel impossibly large to a first-time saver — normalise starting with a 1-month "starter" fund instead
• Emphasise "essential expenses" specifically — many learners will initially include discretionary spending, inflating the target
• The fund must stay liquid — locking it in a fixed deposit or investment defeats its purpose entirely
• Connect back to Chanda's story: her problem wasn't overspending, it was the absence of this specific fund

Core Concept 2

Not Everything Urgent Is an Emergency

A fund only protects you if it's still there when you actually need it. Every time it gets spent on something that isn't a true emergency, it's not protecting you from the shock that's actually coming.
